# Theory Mapping

## Baseline Mechanism From Qian et al.

Qian et al.'s supplier-market-reaction design relies on a downstream innovation signal:

```text
Downstream customer concrete GenAI initiative
-> market updates expectations about future AI-related demand, product redesign, or process reconfiguration
-> pre-existing suppliers may benefit from expected future orders, capability upgrading, or stronger customer growth
-> supplier abnormal return around the announcement date
```

The China replication should keep this logic narrow. The project should not claim that every GenAI keyword disclosure creates real supplier demand.

## China Transfer

The China setting differs from the U.S. newswire setting in three ways:

1. The main auditable source is CNINFO formal disclosure, not newswire releases.
2. Many firm actions appear first through product launches, company releases, qualified securities media, or public events, then later through formal documents.
3. China adoption timing may be slower than the U.S. 2023-2024 window, so `2023-2026` is used as the China extended window, while `2023-2024` remains the U.S.-comparable window.

## Positive Signal Required

The treatment must describe a concrete initiative that plausibly changes economic expectations. Valid examples include:

- launching a GenAI product or service;
- integrating large models into products or workflows;
- investing in GenAI/AI computing projects;
- filing or releasing a model/service;
- signing a strategic cooperation or contract tied to GenAI deployment.

Invalid examples include:

- denying ChatGPT/GenAI business;
- saying the firm is merely paying attention to the technology;
- industry background in annual reports;
- investor questions where the company does not announce an action;
- generic AI or digital transformation discussion.
